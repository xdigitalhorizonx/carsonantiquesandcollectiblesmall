What's in the booths

We make antiquing accessible. This is a multi-vendor mall — numbered booths stocked by different vendors — so every aisle is a different hunt. Six places to start:

  • Collectibles

    A Phillips 66 sign, a California 32 marker, hubcaps and old hand planes. Americana, wall to floor.

  • Home Goods

    Crystal chandeliers, a beaded lace lampshade, pink fringed shades. Home decor, old and newer.

  • China

    Teapots, a blue tea set, cups and saucers on oak shelves. Pattern-matchers, this is your stop.

  • Furniture

    A tall wooden hutch full of dolls, baskets and linens — a single booth. The hutches keep coming.

  • Artwork

    Pencil drawings and old photographs on a booth wall, a vest hung beside them. Art and frames.

  • Currency

    Rows of coins in flip holders in a lit case, under a Coins sign. Collectors, bring your loupe.

Also on the sign out front: Gifts · Home Decor · Art · Toys & More.

Make a day of it in Carson City

We're on North Carson Street, about a mile north of the Capitol — close enough to walk to the old Mint, the Children's Museum and the Capitol itself. Distances are from our front door.

North Carson Street from our door south to the State Capitol, about 0.9 mi. Drawn as a straight line — not to scale beyond the street.

Walk it — down North Carson Street

  1. Children's Museum of Northern Nevada

    813 N Carson St · 0.6 mi south, about a 15-min walk

    Hands-on exhibits for the kids — trade an hour of booth-browsing for an hour of theirs.

  2. Nevada State Museum — the old Carson City Mint

    600 N Carson St · 0.7 mi south, about a 15-min walk

    Coins stamped "CC" were struck here from 1870 to 1893, and the mint's original Coin Press No. 1 still runs on set days. If our Currency booths got you going, this is the next stop.

  3. Nevada State Capitol

    101 N Carson St · 0.9 mi south, about a 20-min walk

    Built 1869–71 from sandstone quarried at the old Nevada State Prison. Pick up the Kit Carson Trail map from Visit Carson City — 2.5 miles of historic homes and landmarks.

Short drives

  • Carson Hot Springs Resort

    1500 Old Hot Springs Rd · 2 mi, about a 10-min drive

    Hours on your feet in the aisles, then a soak.

  • Nevada State Railroad Museum

    2180 S Carson St · 2.2 mi south, about a 10-min drive

    Virginia & Truckee locomotives and cars.

  • Stewart Indian School Cultural Center & Museum

    1 Jacobsen Way · 5 mi south, about a 15-min drive

    The history of the Stewart Indian School, told on its own campus.

Check each place's hours before you go — we only know ours: every day, 10–6.

Questions before you visit

Visiting

What are your hours?
Every day, 10 AM to 6 PM — Monday through Sunday.
Are you open on Sundays?
Yes — every day of the week, Saturday and Sunday included, 10 AM to 6 PM.
Where is Carson Antiques & Collectibles Mall?
1831 N Carson St, Carson City, NV 89701 — on North Carson Street, under the yellow sign.
Is there parking?
Yes — there's a lot right in front of the building.
Are there restrooms?
Yes — they're marked on the Treasure Map.
How far is the mall from Reno?
About 31 miles — roughly 40 minutes south on I-580. We're at 1831 N Carson St, about a mile north of the State Capitol.
How far is the mall from Lake Tahoe?
From Stateline it's about 26 miles, around 45 minutes via US-50. From Incline Village it's about 26 miles, around 50 minutes via NV-28 and US-50. From South Lake Tahoe it's about 30 miles, around 55 minutes via US-50.
What's near the mall?
The Nevada State Museum — the old Carson City Mint — is 0.7 miles south on North Carson Street, about a 15-minute walk. The Children's Museum of Northern Nevada and the State Capitol are on the same stretch. More ideas under Make a day of it in Carson City.
How big is the mall?
Over 21,000 square feet on two floors — Northern Nevada's largest antique mall, as the sign out front says.
Is there an upstairs?
Yes. The Treasure Map on this page shows the main floor; there are more booths upstairs.
Is everything actually antique?
Not everything — and the sign out front says as much: Gifts · Home Decor · Art · Toys & More. This is a multi-vendor mall, so the booths carry antiques, vintage items, collectibles and newer home decor side by side. That mix is the hunt.
How does an antique mall work?
Different vendors rent the numbered booths and stock them with their own finds, so every aisle is a different hunt. Browse at your own pace — the cashier is at the entrance.
